# Reaction Condition Intelligence

How RamChems answers "what reagent, solvent, temperature and time go on that
arrow?" for a retrosynthesis step — and, just as importantly, how it says *"I
don't know"* when it doesn't.

**Status: implemented and tested. Not experimentally validated, and not
commercially cleared** — see [Limitations](#limitations) and
[data-provenance.md](data-provenance.md).

---

## The governing rule

> **Traceability over completeness.** A missing answer is better than
> fabricated chemistry.

Nothing in this subsystem invents a paper title, an author, a journal, a DOI, a
URL, a patent number, a yield, a temperature, a catalyst or a solvent. Every
value that reaches the UI came out of an indexed source record and carries a
pointer back to it. Where a source recorded nothing, the field is **absent from
the response** — not null, not zero, not a plausible default.

---

## Why this subsystem exists at all

AiZynthFinder is a *route* engine. Its USPTO template library has five columns
(index, reaction SMARTS, retro template, template hash, library occurrence) and
**no condition data whatsoever**. The reaction SMILES it emits has an empty
agents slot:

```
CC(=O)OC(C)=O.O=C(O)c1ccccc1O>>CC(=O)Oc1ccccc1C(=O)O
                              ↑ nothing between the two > characters
```

So conditions cannot be recovered from the route search at any level of effort.
They have to come from a separate evidence source, matched to the step after
the search finishes. That is what this subsystem is.

**It does not touch the retrosynthesis engine.** AiZynthFinder is not modified,
wrapped or replaced; enrichment runs on the API's own route dict after the
search returns.

## Evidence hierarchy

Four levels, never blurred, distinguished by colour **and** icon **and** wording
in the UI:

| Level | UI label | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `experimental` | 🟢 **DIRECT EXPERIMENTAL PRECEDENT** | This exact transformation, on these substrates, was found in the indexed experimental database |
| `similar_experimental` | 🟡 **SIMILAR EXPERIMENTAL PRECEDENT** | Conditions observed in chemically similar reactions **on different substrates**. Not a precedent for this reaction |
| `predicted` | 🔵 **AI-PREDICTED CONDITIONS** | Generated by a prediction model. Not experimentally verified; nobody ran this |
| `unavailable` | ⚪ **NO VERIFIED CONDITION EVIDENCE** | No suitable experimental evidence found in the indexed sources. Not a statement that none exists |

The service tries them strictly in that order and stops at the first that
yields something. It never falls back to a *higher* claim than the evidence
supports.

### What a precedent is NOT

A matched record is evidence found **after** the route was proposed. It is not
where the step came from. The step came from a USPTO reaction template inside
AiZynthFinder; the evidence layer then searched an index for reactions that
look like it.

So the system may say *"direct experimental precedent found"*. It must never
say, or allow a reader to infer, *"AiZynthFinder took this reaction from Paper
X"*. The expanded evidence panel states this in as many words, above the
records, on every step that has any.

The word "literature" is likewise avoided in user-facing text. Nothing here
searches the literature; it searches a finite indexed corpus. Every "not found"
message names that corpus and its size, and the route-level coverage line does
the same. `literature_sources` in the route summary is therefore named
`distinct_sources` — distinct source records (DOI, patent number, or record id)
behind the matched precedents, counted once each.

`confidence` is structurally forbidden on anything but a prediction —
`ConditionValue.__post_init__` raises `ValueError` if you try. An experimentally
reported 80 °C has no model confidence to report; its strength is expressed by
`evidence_level` and `observation_count`.

## Architecture

```
backend/conditions/
  schema.py        domain model — imports neither AiZynthFinder nor ORD
  normalize.py     reaction identity + similarity
  providers.py     LiteratureProvider / ConditionPredictionProvider interfaces
  ord_provider.py  ORD implementation (reads flat Postgres rows, never protobuf)
  extract.py       unit conversion that never destroys the reported value
  aggregate.py     combine across precedents without inventing a consensus
  service.py       orchestration + Postgres cache
```

The dependency direction matters: `schema.py` knows nothing about where
evidence comes from, and `ord_provider.py` is the only file that knows ORD
exists. Adding Lowe/USPTO, an internal ELN, a patent corpus or a commercial
database means writing **one class** implementing `LiteratureProvider` — no
change to normalisation, aggregation, the API or the frontend.

### Reaction identity

```python
reaction_key = sha256("<sorted canonical reactants>>><sorted canonical products>")
```

Deliberately **not** the template hash. One template spans many substrates —
template 40152 covers every aromatic ester formation from an anhydride — so a
template-keyed lookup would return precedents for entirely different molecules
and label them "experimental". Identity is substrate-specific.

Reactants are canonicalised and sorted, so `A + B` and `B + A` collapse to one
key. Agents are excluded: the same transformation in THF or DMF is the same
reaction for matching purposes, and the agents are precisely what we are looking
up.

Canonicalisation here does **not** route through
`backend.molrepr.service.canonicalize`. That applies parent-compound stripping
for the search library, which would discard salts and counter-ions that
genuinely take part in a reaction.

### Retrieval — two stages

1. **Prefilter in Postgres.** The RDKit cartridge's GiST-indexed Morgan
   fingerprint on the major product (`product_bfp`), threshold 0.5, cap 400
   candidates. Cheap, and avoids scanning 216k rows in Python.
2. **Rank in Python** on the reaction *difference* fingerprint
   (`CreateDifferenceFingerprintForReaction`), which keys on what **changes**
   between reactants and products rather than on how alike the molecules look:

   ```
   score = 0.30 × transformation_similarity + 0.70 × substrate_similarity
   ```

   Those weights, the `0.4` prefilter threshold and the `0.20` similarity floor
   are all **measured**, not guessed — see
   [retrieval-benchmark.md](retrieval-benchmark.md). The original guesses
   (`0.65/0.35`, threshold `0.5`, floor `0.35`) were wrong in every case.

Neither stage is sufficient alone. Stage 1 alone returns molecules that merely
*resemble* the product; stage 2 alone would require scanning everything. Weights
live in `ord_provider.py` as named constants — tune them there, not at the call
site.

Measured behaviour of the difference fingerprint: two esterifications of
different acids score 0.419 transformation / 0.727 substrate; an unrelated
nucleophilic substitution scores 0.0.

### Aggregation

When several precedents match, `aggregate.py` reports the **median** plus the
**observed range** and the observation count — never a single "recommended"
number.

Median rather than mean because reaction datasets carry outliers (a 200 °C entry
among room-temperature runs) that drag a mean. Chemicals are ranked by how many
precedents used them, counted **once per precedent**, and rendered as
`THF (7/10 precedents)`.

The note attached to every aggregate says so explicitly: *"Frequencies and
ranges describe what was reported, not a recommended procedure."*

### Caching

`reaction_evidence_cache` in the existing PostgreSQL instance — **no Redis**.
The cache key is:

```
(reaction_key, provider, provider_version, dataset_version)
```

`dataset_version` is content-addressed (sha256 of the source parquet, truncated),
aggregated across all ingested datasets. Refresh a dataset and the version
string changes, which changes the key, which retires stale evidence instead of
letting it look current. Same for `provider_version` when retrieval logic
changes.

A cache that cannot be read or written is a *slow* cache, not a failure — every
cache path is wrapped and falls through to live retrieval.

Both the cache-hit and fresh paths return the same **transport dict**. An
earlier draft rebuilt dataclasses from cached JSON and silently dropped
conditions on the hit path; the two paths are now the same type by construction,
and `test_second_lookup_is_served_from_cache` asserts the contents match.

## Failure behaviour

**A route that solved stays solved.** Full stop.

Enrichment runs *after* the search, outside the engine, and every step is
individually wrapped:

- Evidence database down → each step reports `unavailable` with a reason.
- Provider missing or never ingested → `NullProvider`, everything `unavailable`.
- One step fails to normalise → that step is `unavailable`, the others are fine.
- `ConditionsService` itself fails to construct → enrichment is skipped entirely.

`is_solved`, `solved_routes_found`, `routes` and `number_of_reactions` are
identical with and without `include_conditions`. This is asserted by
`test_conditions_do_not_change_whether_a_route_is_solved` and, for the negative
case, `test_ibuprofen_stays_unsolved_when_conditions_are_requested` — ibuprofen
does not solve at the default 100 iterations, and five reported precedents on a
step must not change that. "Solved" is a statement about purchasable starting
material, not about literature support.

## API

### `POST /retrosynthesis/plan`

```jsonc
{ "smiles": "...", "top_n": 5, "include_conditions": false }
```

`include_conditions` defaults to **false**. With it off the response is byte-
identical to the pre-evidence shape — no `evidence` key on reactions, no
`evidence_summary` on routes — and costs the same.

Measured overhead with it on, after subtracting the MCTS search time (which
dominates and varies run to run):

| Step outcome | Cold lookup | Cached |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct match (exact key hit) | +250 ms – 1.4 s | +20 ms |
| Similar match (400-candidate rerank) | +250 ms – 1.4 s | +12 ms |
| No match | +350 ms | +44 ms |

The cold cost is dominated by stage 2: up to `PREFILTER_LIMIT` = 400 candidate
reactions are re-normalised and fingerprinted in Python per step. That is the
first thing to optimise if the flag is ever made default-on.

> An earlier version of this document claimed ~10 ms. That figure was measured
> while the similar-precedent path was silently returning `[]` (see
> [Known defects](#known-defects-found-and-fixed)), so it was timing a code path
> that did nothing.

With it on, every reaction gains `evidence` and every route gains:

```json
{
  "steps": 3,
  "steps_with_experimental_evidence": 1,
  "steps_with_similar_evidence": 1,
  "steps_predicted": 0,
  "steps_without_evidence": 1,
  "evidence_coverage": 0.667,
  "literature_sources": 2
}
```

`evidence_coverage` describes how much of the route appears in the **indexed
evidence sources**. It is not a feasibility, yield or success estimate, and a
step with no precedent may still be perfectly good chemistry that simply is not
in the indexed datasets.

`evidence_coverage` and `distinct_sources` describe how much of the route
appears in the **indexed evidence sources**. Not a feasibility, yield or success
estimate; a step with no match may still be well precedented outside the index.

### `POST /retrosynthesis/conditions`

Evidence for one reaction, independent of any route search.

```jsonc
{ "reactants": ["COS(=O)(=O)OC", "Fc1cc(Br)cc2cn[nH]c12"],
  "products":  ["Cn1ncc2cc(Br)cc(F)c21"] }
```

Always returns a body. "Nothing found" is `200` with
`evidence_level: "unavailable"` and a reason — not a 404 and not a 500.
Unparseable input is `400`.

### `GET /retrosynthesis/evidence/status`

Provider, availability, dataset version, **data licence**, the size of the
indexed corpus (`indexed_reactions`), a `coverage_note` stating that this is a
finite index rather than a literature search, and whether a condition-prediction
model is configured. The licence travels with the data so a consumer cannot use
the evidence without being shown the terms.

```json
{
  "provider": "ord",
  "provider_display_name": "Open Reaction Database (indexed subset)",
  "provider_version": "3",
  "available": true,
  "indexed_reactions": 216681,
  "data_license": "CC-BY-SA-4.0 (Open Reaction Database)"
}
```

`provider_version` is part of the evidence cache key. **Bump it whenever
retrieval logic or user-facing wording changes**, or cached rows will keep
serving the old behaviour — this was learned the hard way, twice.

## Frontend

`frontend/src/workspaces/results.tsx` (the live workspace —
`components/RetrosynthesisTab.tsx` is dead code and was left alone).

- **On the arrow**: evidence badge, then reagents / catalyst / solvent /
  temperature / time. Ranges shown as `82.5 C (observed 20–200 C)`.
- **Expandable panel**: the aggregate, then every precedent with its own
  conditions, similarity, dataset, DOI, patent number and a clickable link —
  either one the source record supplied, or, for patent precedents, a Google
  Patents link built from the verified patent number and labelled "link built
  from the patent number" (see data-provenance.md §1b). A DOI is never turned
  into a URL by string concatenation; a DOI-only record renders "No link
  supplied by the source record; DOI shown exactly as recorded."
- **Route header**: the coverage strip with all four counts and the caveat that
  coverage plays no part in whether a route is solved.
- **Template occurrence** is surfaced in the reaction detail, labelled "how many
  times this template appears in the USPTO template library. A library count,
  not a count of successful experiments, a yield or a probability."
- `similarity` is tooltipped as "Chemical similarity between the two
  transformations. NOT a probability that this reaction will work."

The **Literature conditions** checkbox in the retrosynthesis options controls
`include_conditions`; it defaults to on in the UI while the API default stays
off.

## Adding another evidence provider

1. Implement `LiteratureProvider` in `backend/conditions/`: `name`, `version`,
   `dataset_version`, `available`, `find_exact_precedents`,
   `find_similar_precedents`, `get_details`.
2. Return `Precedent` objects with a populated `Provenance`. Leave unknown
   provenance fields `None` — do not fill them in.
3. Register it in `ConditionsService._default_provider` (or pass it in; the
   constructor takes a provider and a pool factory, which is how the tests run
   without a database).

Candidates, in rough order of value:

| Source | Carries | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lowe USPTO (1976–2016) | agents, yield, patent number | ~1.8 M reactions, but **no temperature or time** |
| Internal ELN / research data | everything, and it's yours | highest value; no licence problem |
| Reaxys / CAS / Pistachio | everything | commercial licence required |
| Patent full text | procedures as prose | needs extraction, which needs its own provenance story |

## Adding a condition-prediction model

There is deliberately **no** prediction model wired in.
`UnavailablePredictionProvider` returns `None` and says why: the strong
published condition recommenders (e.g. Gao et al. as shipped in ASKCOS) are
trained on licensed reaction data and distributed under non-commercial terms,
which does not fit a product intended for commercial use. Shipping one would
have imported a licence problem; fabricating numbers instead was not an option.

A replacement must meet all of:

- a licence compatible with commercial use, **for the weights and the training
  data**;
- outputs expressible as `ReactionConditions` with
  `evidence_level = PREDICTED`;
- a calibrated confidence, since `PREDICTED` is the only level allowed to carry
  one;
- local inference — see [Privacy](#privacy).

Until then the honest answer is ⚪, and that is what the UI shows.

## Privacy

- **Nothing in the serving path opens a socket.** Asserted by
  `test_the_evidence_layer_makes_no_outbound_network_calls`, which fails the
  build if `requests`, `urllib.request`, `httpx`, `aiohttp` or raw sockets
  appear anywhere in `backend/conditions/`.
- Ingestion **does** download from Hugging Face — a deliberate, offline,
  operator-run step in a separate conda env, never triggered by a user request.
- No log line in the evidence path interpolates a SMILES or a reaction key,
  asserted by `test_proprietary_structures_are_not_written_to_logs`.
- Retrieval is entirely local: your molecules are compared against a local
  Postgres index and never leave the machine.

## Environment isolation

Ingestion runs in its own conda env, `ord-ingest`:

```bash
conda activate ord-ingest
python scripts/ingest_ord.py --list
python scripts/ingest_ord.py --dataset <id> [--limit N]
```

`ord-schema` pins `protobuf<6` while the serving env runs protobuf 7 under
onnxruntime, and it pulls `rdkit>=2026` against AiZynthFinder's `rdkit<2024`.
Installing it into `retrosynth` would have downgraded protobuf to 5.29.6 and
broken the ONNX runtime. The serving environment therefore never imports
`ord-schema` at all — it only reads the flat rows the ingest script writes.

## Known defects, found and fixed

Recorded because each one was invisible from the outside, and the mechanism that
hid them is worth remembering.

### 1. Enum members serialised as Python reprs

`asdict()` left `SourceType` / `EvidenceLevel` members in place, so a JSON
column received `"<SourceType.DATASET: 'dataset'>"`. Fixed by unwrapping enums
in `_drop_none`; the stale cache was truncated. Guarded by
`test_no_enum_repr_ever_reaches_the_payload`.

### 2. Exact matches were tagged `similar_reaction`

Aggregates over **direct** precedents claimed `source_type =
SourceType.SIMILAR_REACTION` — understating the evidence, and blurring exactly
the distinction that matters most. Fixed with `_source_type_for()`. Guarded by
`test_direct_evidence_is_not_tagged_as_a_similar_reaction`.

### 3. The similar-precedent path never ran at all

**The most serious one.** Two stacked bugs in three lines:

1. `SET LOCAL rdkit.tanimoto_threshold = …` raised `UndefinedObject`, because
   the cartridge registers its GUCs only once its shared library is loaded into
   the backend — which happens on first use of an rdkit function in that
   session.
2. Even with the parameter registered, plain `SET` takes no bind parameter, so
   `SET LOCAL … = %s` is a syntax error.

Both were swallowed by a bare `except Exception: return []`, so
`find_similar_precedents` returned an empty list **every time, for the life of
the first implementation**. Every step that should have reported
`similar_experimental` reported `unavailable` instead.

Fixed by touching an rdkit function first and using `set_config(…, true)`, which
does accept parameters. The bare swallow now logs a warning — silence was what
made this survive a full test suite and a manual end-to-end validation.

Impact of the fix, on the same targets: aspirin went from "no evidence" to 6
similar precedents; paracetamol from "no evidence" to 1 direct precedent.
Guarded by `test_similar_precedent_retrieval_is_not_silently_dead`, which uses a
one-atom-different near miss and asserts the similar path returns something.

### 4. Empty aggregates asserted observations that never happened

ORD contains plenty of reactions with no temperature, time, solvent or reagent.
Aggregating six of those produced a conditions block whose only content was
*"Observed across 6 experimental precedents"* — asserting observations that were
never made. `aggregate()` now returns `UNAVAILABLE` conditions when everything
came out empty, and the service explains: *"6 similar reactions found, but none
of those records include reaction conditions. The transformation is attested;
the conditions are not."*

### The common thread

Three of the four were hidden by code that caught an exception and returned an
empty result. "Degrade gracefully" and "fail silently" are one line apart. Every
swallow in this subsystem now logs.

## Limitations

**Implemented and tested** — the subsystem works end to end, 80 tests cover it,
and the behaviour described above is what the code does.

**Not experimentally validated** — no condition returned by this system has been
run in a lab by us. The numbers are what a source record reported for *its*
substrates under *its* conditions. Scale, purity, equipment and the exact
substrate all change real chemistry.

**Not commercially cleared** — the indexed data is CC-BY-SA-4.0 (ShareAlike).
See [data-provenance.md](data-provenance.md) before shipping anything derived
from it. The provider should be treated as a **development / research evidence
source, commercial clearance pending**, not as a permanent foundation.

Concrete gaps:

- **Coverage is thin.** 216,681 reactions from 6 datasets, out of ~3 M in ORD
  and vastly more in the literature. Many targets return ⚪, and the honest
  answer is "not in the index", not a guess. A data-volume problem, not a code
  problem — but it means the UI must never imply comprehensive coverage, which
  is why every message names the corpus and its size.
- **HTE bias.** Several ingested datasets are high-throughput screening plates,
  so hundreds of precedents may share one DOI and one procedure. `literature_
  sources` counts distinct DOIs precisely so this is visible.
- **Partial DOIs.** Some ORD records store a truncated DOI (`10.1038/s41557`).
  It is displayed exactly as recorded; it is not repaired, extended or guessed.
- **Yields are per-record.** A precedent's yield describes that experiment, not
  a yield you should expect.
- **No condition prediction.** Steps with no precedent get ⚪, never a model
  output, until a commercially licensable model exists.
- **Retrieval quality is now benchmarked, and it is mediocre.** Against ORD's
  own reaction-type labels: P@1 0.516, +0.158 over random, 28% of queries answered
  with nothing. Conditional on stage 1 surfacing any relevant reaction at all,
  the ranking puts a correct-transformation reaction first **92%** of the time —
  so the ranking is good and **recall is the problem**. Stage 1 offers a relevant
  candidate for only 56% of queries, because it retrieves by product-molecule
  similarity, which is a poor proxy for "same transformation". Full method,
  numbers and caveats in [retrieval-benchmark.md](retrieval-benchmark.md).
- **Stage 2 recomputes fingerprints per request**, so cost scales with candidate
  count and forces a worse prefilter threshold than the benchmark prefers.
  Precomputing candidate fingerprints into Postgres is the unlock.
- **Names are not resolved to structures.** "TEA" stays "TEA" with
  `smiles: null`, rather than becoming triethanolamine when the paper meant
  triethylamine.
